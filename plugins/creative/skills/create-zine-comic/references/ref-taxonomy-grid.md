# Page 5: When to Build a Skill — Trust Your Frustration

A landscape-format educational comic book page (2048x1440px) with a clean white/light cream background and a structured 4-column × 2-row grid layout, giving us 8 slots to work with. Panels can be combined for emphasis — this is a comic book, and the artist has freedom to merge slots for impact.

The style is polished, modern comic book — clean lines, vibrant colors, professional typography mixed with handwritten-style annotations. Sophisticated and well-designed. Comic book meets beautiful iPad notes.

IMPORTANT: Do NOT number the panels. No circled numbers or digits in the top-left corners of panels. The layout should flow naturally without explicit numbering.

## The Star: Claudie

The recurring character is "Claudie" — a small, cute, blocky orange pixel-art creature with four short legs, two square black eyes with small eyelashes, and often holding a small clipboard. See the attached reference image for the exact character design. Claudie is friendly, competent, and slightly amused. Appears across panels as the guide/narrator.

## Goal of This Page

Shift from "what skills are" to "when should I make one." The first half gives a quick overview of the 9 types (so readers know the range of what's possible), but the real message is in the second half: you don't start with a taxonomy — you start with a feeling. The feeling of "I keep teaching my AI the same thing" or "I don't trust my AI with this task" is the signal. That frustration is the raw material for a skill.

### Slot 5 (bottom-left, 1 slot) — "The Real Starting Point"
A dramatic tonal shift. The warm grid gives way to a panel with a slightly darker, more intimate feel — like a conversation getting real.

Bold text at top: "Forget the grid."

Below, in larger handwritten-style text: "You start with a feeling."

A simple illustration: a person at their computer, slightly hunched, with a visible frustration aura (squiggly lines above their head, furrowed brow). They're looking at their AI chat and the same mistake is happening again. A thought bubble: "I JUST told you this yesterday..."

Claudie sits nearby, clipboard out, pointing at the person with a gentle "that's the signal" gesture.

### Slots 6-7 (bottom-center, merged 2 slots) — "Three Signals You Should Build a Skill"
Three illustrated scenarios stacked vertically, each with a bold signal statement and a relatable scene:

**Signal 1: "You keep teaching the same lesson."**
Illustration: A comic strip of three identical chat exchanges — the user explaining the same convention to their AI in three different sessions. Monday: "Remember, we use camelCase..." Wednesday: "No, camelCase, not snake_case..." Friday: "I told you, camelCase!" The AI has a blank, fresh expression each time — it doesn't remember.
Annotation: "If you've explained it three times, it's a skill."

**Signal 2: "You don't trust the AI with this task."**
Illustration: A person hovering over their AI's shoulder, wincing. The AI is about to do something — deploy, send an email, modify a database — and the person's hands are outstretched like they're ready to grab the steering wheel. Thought bubble: "It doesn't understand how we do this..."
Annotation: "Low trust = missing knowledge. A skill fills the gap."

**Signal 3: "You keep making the same corrections."**
Illustration: A person with a red pen, marking up AI output for the Nth time. The same types of corrections appear repeatedly — wrong format, wrong style, wrong assumptions. A growing pile of corrected outputs stacks up beside them.
Annotation: "Every correction is a lesson that should be permanent."

### Slot 8 (bottom-right, 1 slot) — "The Reframe"
A warm, encouraging panel. Orange-cream background. The emotional payoff.

Bold text at top: "Your corrections compound."

A visual metaphor: A funnel or jar labeled "SKILL" at the top. Flowing into it from above are small slips of paper, each one a correction or lesson: "use camelCase," "check for null first," "always validate input," "deploy to staging before prod." The jar is filling up over time.

Below the jar: "Right now, your lessons evaporate between sessions. A skill captures them permanently. Each correction makes it smarter."

A small equation-style visual:
"Frustration + 5 minutes = A skill that never forgets"

Claudie at the bottom, looking at the reader warmly: "That thing that keeps annoying you? That's your first skill."

## Visual Design Notes

- Thin light gray grid lines separating panels, with 8px rounded corners on each panel
- The page has a clear tonal arc: structured/encyclopedic (the 9-type grid) → intimate/emotional (the frustration signals) → empowering (the reframe)
- The 3x3 grid should be clean and scannable — the reader shouldn't linger here, it's reference material. Icons do the heavy lifting.
- The three signals in slots 6-7 should feel like comic strip vignettes — small, relatable, slightly humorous scenes of AI frustration
- The corrections/frustration imagery should be universal — anyone who's used AI extensively will recognize these moments
- The "corrections compound" metaphor in slot 8 should feel satisfying — like seeing a savings account grow
- Color shift: top half is organized/cool (blues, greens, purples of the grid), bottom half is warmer (the human, emotional territory)
- NO panel numbers anywhere — the layout flows naturally
- Same visual style and feel as the rest of the Claude Code Zines series
