# Page 1: You Already Do This — From Saved Prompts to Superpowers

A landscape-format educational comic book page (2048x1440px) with a clean white/light cream background and a structured 4-column × 2-row grid layout, giving us 8 slots to work with. Panels can be combined for emphasis — this is a comic book, and the artist has freedom to merge slots for impact.

The style is polished, modern comic book — clean lines, vibrant colors, professional typography mixed with handwritten-style annotations. Sophisticated and well-designed. Comic book meets beautiful iPad notes.

IMPORTANT: Do NOT number the panels. No circled numbers or digits in the top-left corners of panels. The layout should flow naturally without explicit numbering.

## The Star: Claudie

The recurring character is "Claudie" — a small, cute, blocky orange pixel-art creature with four short legs, two square black eyes with small eyelashes, and often holding a small clipboard. See the attached reference image for the exact character design. Claudie is friendly, competent, and slightly amused. Appears across panels as the guide/narrator.

## Goal of This Page

Empathy first, then the hook. The reader should feel SEEN — "oh, they're talking about me" — because they've been saving prompts in Google Docs, ChatGPT custom instructions, or Claude projects. Then we show the limitation: these saved prompts are passive, YOU carry the load. And then the payoff: the Matrix moment. What if your AI could load expertise on demand? That's the shift from saved prompts to skills.

## Panel Layout

### Slot 1 (top-left, 1 slot) — Title Panel
Bold comic-style title "You Already Do This" in large dark charcoal lettering with an orange accent underline. Subtitle in smaller, friendlier text: "...you just don't know it has a name yet." Claudie leans against the edge of the title text casually, clipboard tucked under one arm, looking directly at the reader with a knowing half-smile — like someone who's about to tell you something that'll change how you think.

### Slots 2-3 (top-center + top-right, merged 2 slots) — "The Prompt Hoarder"
A warm, relatable collage showing THREE ways people save their prompts today, arranged side by side like snapshots pinned to a board:

**Snapshot 1:** A Google Doc titled "My AI Prompts" — visible lines of text like "For code review, use this:", "When writing emails, paste this:", with yellow highlighted sections and messy formatting. The doc has 47 pages. A small annotation arrow points to the page count: "It started as one page..."

**Snapshot 2:** A ChatGPT interface showing the "Custom Instructions" panel open, crammed with text — instructions stuffed into both the "What would you like ChatGPT to know?" and "How would you like ChatGPT to respond?" boxes, text running to the character limit. A sticky note overlaid reads: "Also works: Claude Projects, system prompts, pinned messages..."

**Snapshot 3:** A code editor showing a file at `.claude/commands/deploy.md` — a simple markdown file with a prompt inside. A handwritten annotation: "Claude Code's first attempt: save prompts as text files." Below it: "Type /deploy → prompt gets injected. That's it. A text expander."

At the bottom of this merged panel, a warm caption in handwritten style: "If any of this looks familiar — good. You've been doing the smart thing." Claudie peeks out from behind the Google Doc snapshot, giving a thumbs up.

### Slot 4 (top-right, 1 slot) — "But Here's the Problem"
A panel with a slightly cooler color temperature — pale blue-gray background to contrast with the warm collage. Claudie stands in the center looking thoughtful, clipboard held up.

Bold text at the top: "But YOU carry all the weight."

Three short lines below, each with a small red "x" icon:
- "You have to REMEMBER the prompt exists"
- "You have to FIND it and copy-paste it"  
- "Your AI has no idea it's there"

At the bottom, slightly smaller italic text: "Your prompts are smart. But they're passive. They sit in a doc until you go get them."

A visual metaphor: a small illustration of a person carrying a heavy stack of folders/documents on their back, walking toward a computer screen that shows a blank AI chat. The AI is just... waiting. It doesn't know the person has all this knowledge.

### Slots 5-7 (bottom-left + bottom-center + bottom-center-right, merged 3 slots) — "The Matrix Moment"
This is the BIG panel — the visual centerpiece of the page. A stylized, comic-book homage to the classic sci-fi idea of "instant learning":

The scene: A futuristic learning station. In the center, a glowing chair surrounded by floating holographic screens. A person sits in the chair, eyes wide open with amazement, as streams of colorful knowledge visually flow into their mind — represented as glowing ribbons of light swirling around their head (like an aurora). The effect is magical and beautiful, not medical or mechanical.

The floating holographic screens around the chair display program/skill names loading: "Code Review," "Deploy to Production," "Write API Docs," "Debug Memory Leaks," "Database Migration," "Brand Guidelines." Each screen has a progress bar showing the skill being absorbed. Some show "LOADED ✓" in green.

A second figure stands at a sleek console nearby, selecting which skills to load — their hand hovers over a glowing menu, choosing the next cassette tape from a rack.

At the bottom of the panel, large bold text in a cinematic font: "What if your AI could just... load expertise?"

Below that, slightly smaller: "Not copy-paste a prompt. Not follow instructions you remembered to give it. Actually LOAD a complete capability — and know when to use it on its own."

Claudie sits in the corner of this panel on a small stool, eating popcorn, watching the scene. A tiny speech bubble: "This is the good part."

### Slot 8 (bottom-right, 1 slot) — "That's What Skills Are"
A clean, punchy reveal panel. Warm orange-cream background matching Claudie's color.

At the top, bold text: "That's what Skills are."

Below, three short lines with green checkmark icons, directly answering the red x's from Slot 4:
- "Your AI KNOWS what's available"
- "It loads the right one AUTOMATICALLY"
- "It becomes an expert ON DEMAND"

A visual metaphor at the center: a small illustration of a rack of cassette tapes (retro, colorful, each labeled with a skill name like "Code Review," "Testing," "Deployment"), and a hand (or Claudie's little arm) pulling one out and inserting it into a slot on the side of a computer/AI brain.

Below the cassette illustration, in friendly handwritten text: "Mini-courses your AI can take instantly. A library of expertise it can reach for whenever it needs."

Claudie at the bottom, looking at the reader with arms spread wide: "Ready to see how they work?"

## Visual Design Notes

- Thin light gray grid lines separating panels, with 8px rounded corners on each panel
- Color palette: warm oranges and creams for the empathy panels (slots 1-3), cooler blue-gray for the "problem" panel (slot 4), cinematic dark tones with green Matrix code for the big panel (slots 5-7), warm orange for the reveal (slot 8)
- The Matrix panel should feel CINEMATIC — darker, moodier, more dramatic than the rest of the page. It's the emotional peak.
- The transition from warm (you're smart) → cool (but there's a problem) → cinematic (the vision) → warm again (here's the answer) creates an emotional arc across one page
- Typography mixes clean sans-serif for main text with casual handwritten font for annotations
- The cassette tape rack in slot 8 should feel tangible and fun — colorful tapes with hand-lettered labels
- NO panel numbers anywhere — the layout flows naturally
- Same visual style and feel as the rest of the Claude Code Zines series
